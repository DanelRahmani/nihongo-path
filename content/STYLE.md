# Lesson style guide

Lessons blend two teaching styles the learner finds easiest:

**Cure Dolly (Organic Japanese)** — explain Japanese *as Japanese*, not as broken English.
- Every sentence has a **core sentence**: a が-subject + a verb, adjective, or noun+だ.
- The subject is always there, even when unsaid: the **zero が** (e.g. `パン を [食|た]べる` → zero-が "I").
- は is **not** the subject marker. It sets the **topic** from outside the core sentence
  ("as for X…"); it is exclusive/contrastive. も is the inclusive topic ("X too").
- Particles are the engine: they tell you each word's job, so order can move while the verb stays last.

**Jouzu Juls** — practical and example-first.
- Lead with natural, everyday sentences people actually say; flag textbook-only phrasing.
- Minimal pairs: change one thing, say plainly how the feeling changes.
- Name the common mistake and the fix. Keep jokes light, jargon rare.

## Rules for content
- Original examples only; never copy from videos or textbooks.
- Every kanji goes in `[漢字|かな]` markup (Kana-only mode must never show a bare kanji).
- Separate words with spaces and write particles as their own word — romaji depends on it.
- Each lesson cites a `source` the rule was checked against (Tae Kim, Genki, IMABI, Cure Dolly).
- English translations show nuance ("It's *Japan* that is beautiful"), not just word-for-word gloss.

## Notes from the learner's Notion (checked)
Useful framing kept: zero が, core sentence, は exclusive / も inclusive topic, に target vs で place-of-action/means.
Corrected, not copied:
- に has no fixed "time → destination → intention → target" order; time tends to come first by habit only.
- 日本が美しい is grammatical but means "It's *Japan* that's beautiful"; neutral is 日本は美しい.
- 世界で一番ラーメン needs the adjective: 世界で一番おいしいラーメン.
